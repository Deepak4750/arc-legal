# Privacy Policy

Version 4. Effective 27 November 2026.

Arc (formerly Flow) is an app for tasks, water, fitness and nutrition, made by M. Deepak Reddy, an individual developer in India who publishes as Arc Wellness ("we", "us"). We decide how the data described here is used. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use Arc. It is written to meet India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 and its Rules. Arc is not offered in the European Economic Area or the United Kingdom. Some folder and link names still use the old name, Flow.

## The short version

- Your Arc content (tasks, water, workouts, food, body measurements, photos, routes and settings) is stored only on your phone.
- We do not run any server or database that holds your Arc content. We never receive it, so it cannot leak from us.
- A few features use the internet. Each one is listed below with what is sent and to whom.
- Arc's usage and crash data is shared only if you turn it on. You can turn it off any time.
- A bug report is sent only when you choose to send it, after you see what it contains.

## 1. What stays on your phone

Everything you enter or create in Arc is saved in Arc's private storage on your phone:

- Tasks, reminders, schedules and completion history.
- Water goals and intake.
- Fitness routines, workouts, sets, weights, records, notes and rest settings.
- Nutrition logs, foods, recipes and targets (Macros).
- Body details you choose to enter: height, weight, age or birth year, sex, activity level, goals, body measurements and progress photos.
- Cardio sessions, including GPS routes, pace and distance.
- Step counts, including steps read from Health Connect.
- Your profile name, nickname, app settings and the Google account details described in section 3.

We have no copy of any of this. If you uninstall Arc, clear its data or lose your phone, we cannot recover it for you.

## 2. Copies you create yourself

Some features write files outside Arc's private storage. These files are in your control, not ours.

- Keep data on uninstall: when this is on, Arc keeps a copy of its database in the Documents/Flow folder on your phone so your data can come back after a reinstall. Other apps with storage access, and anyone with access to your phone, may be able to read this file. You can turn it off in Settings.
- Export: a zip of your tasks, water and workouts that you save or share where you choose.
- Stickers and shared images: images you choose to share through other apps.
- Import: Hevy or Strong files you choose to open are read on your phone and are not sent anywhere.

Once a file leaves Arc (shared, uploaded, copied or backed up by your phone or another app), the rules of wherever you put it apply.

## 3. Google sign-in

Arc asks you to sign in with Google. Google confirms who you are and gives Arc your Google account ID, email address, name and profile picture link. Arc keeps these on your phone to tell accounts apart. After you agree to the Terms and this policy, Arc also signs you in to its own Google Firebase project (Firebase Authentication), which stores your Google account ID, email address, name and profile picture link so your account is recognised on a new phone. Your tasks, food, workouts, body details, photos and routes are never sent. Google's handling of your sign-in is covered by Google's Privacy Policy.

## 4. Things that use the internet

- Google sign-in: your sign-in request, to Google, when you sign in.
- Account: your Google account ID, email address, name and profile picture link, to Google Firebase Authentication, after you agree to the Terms and this policy.
- Remote settings: a request for current app settings, such as the latest version, to Google Firebase Remote Config, when Arc starts.
- Update notices: a push token for your phone, to Google Firebase Cloud Messaging, only if update notices are on.
- Usage data: app version, device model, Android version, country, a random identifier created by Google's Firebase software, and which Arc features were used. It includes broad groups such as a short, medium or long workout or a gain, hold or lose goal (never what you entered). For reminders that encourage you to come back, it includes which message was shown, opened or swiped away, the hour of the day, and broad groups for how recently you used Arc and how long your streak is. This goes to Google Firebase Analytics, only if you turn it on.
- Crash reports: technical details of a crash (app version, device, Android version, the screen open and the error), to Google Firebase Crashlytics, only if you turn it on.
- Bug reports: your message and, only if you leave them ticked, a screenshot, device details and Arc's recent technical log, to us, stored in Google Firebase Firestore, only when you choose to send one.
- Maps: the map area being shown, which reveals your approximate location, to OpenFreeMap tile servers, while a cardio map is on screen or when Arc draws a map for a sticker you make.
- Barcode lookup: the barcode number, to Open Food Facts, only when you scan a barcode and lookup is on.
- Text reading and barcode scanning: reading a routine from a picture and scanning a barcode use Google's ML Kit and code scanner on your phone. Your pictures are not sent. When you use these features, Google receives device and app details, an installation identifier, and performance and error information, to diagnose problems and measure use. This happens even when usage data is off.
- Copies installed outside Google Play only: a request for the latest version file, and the app or add-on download, to GitHub (owned by Microsoft), when Arc checks for updates or you choose to download. The Google Play version updates through Google Play.

Google's Firebase software also creates a random installation identifier so that these Google services can work. Any internet request reveals your IP address to the service that receives it. We do not combine any of this with your Arc content, because we never have your Arc content.

Usage data and crash reports never include your tasks, food, workouts, body details, photos, routes, name or email address, and we do not link them to your Google account.

## 5. Permissions Arc asks for

- Notifications and exact alarms: to deliver task, water and workout reminders on time.
- Full-screen alarms: so a task alarm can ring over the lock screen.
- Notification access (Workout music): held by the separate Arc Music Access add-on, only to read what is playing (song, artist, cover) and control play, pause and skip in your music app. It does not read or store your other notifications.
- Nearby devices (Workout music): to list your paired headphones and see when they connect. Not used to find your location.
- Ignore battery optimisation: so reminders are not delayed or dropped.
- Start at boot: to restore reminders after the phone restarts.
- Camera: only when you take a photo for a sticker, a progress photo or a barcode scan. Photos stay on your phone.
- Location: only during a cardio session you start, to record your route.
- Physical activity and Health Connect: only if you allow them, to count steps, including in the background. Step data stays on your phone.
- Display over other apps: for task alarms that pop up over other apps, to return to Arc after opening your music app, and, if you turn it on, the companion bubble on your home screen. A quiet notification shows while the companion is on.
- Install apps (copies installed outside Google Play only): to install updates and the add-on you choose to download.
- Internet: for the features in section 4.
- Network state, vibration, keep awake, background services and push messages: to keep timers, tracking and music controls running, to send only when connected and to deliver update notices.

You can withdraw any permission in Android settings. The feature that needs it will stop working.

## 6. Why we process data, and your consent

We process the small amount of data in section 4 only with your consent, given in the app, for these purposes: signing you in, keeping the app working and current, fixing crashes and bugs, and understanding which features and reminders are used so Arc can be improved. We do not sell, rent or trade data. We do not show ads. We do not use your data to train AI models. Arc's command features (home-screen shortcuts and the flow://cmd link) run on your phone and only control Arc.

You can withdraw consent at any time:

- Usage and crash data: Settings → Privacy and permissions.
- Update notices: Settings → Privacy and permissions.
- Bug reports: simply don't send one.
- Your account: delete it in Settings, or see [Delete your Arc account](delete-account.html).

Withdrawing consent does not affect anything done before you withdrew it.

## 7. How long data is kept

- Content on your phone: until you delete it, clear Arc's data or uninstall Arc (the Keep data copy stays until you delete it or turn the option off).
- Account details in Firebase Authentication: until you delete your account or ask us to.
- Usage data: kept by Google Analytics for 2 months, then deleted.
- Crash reports: kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days.
- Bug reports: kept until the bug is resolved, and never longer than 12 months.

## 8. Where data goes

We are in India. Google, GitHub, OpenFreeMap and Open Food Facts may process the data they receive in countries other than yours, including the United States, where data protection laws may differ from yours. Write to us if you want to know more.

## 9. Security

Your content is protected by Android's app sandbox and by your phone's own security (screen lock and encryption). On top of that, Arc encrypts its own database and your photos on the phone with a key kept in your phone's secure hardware, so a copy taken off the phone cannot be read. Exports you make yourself, and the Keep data backup in Documents, are not encrypted, so they can be opened on a new phone. Data sent to Google services travels over encrypted connections. Because your content stays on your phone, its safety depends on your phone: keep a screen lock, keep Android updated, and only install apps you trust. See the Terms and Conditions for responsibilities.

## 10. Your rights

Under India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 you can:

- Ask what personal data of yours we process, and ask for it to be corrected, completed, updated or erased.
- Withdraw consent at any time.
- Nominate someone to exercise your rights if you die or become unable to.
- Raise a grievance with us, and if you are not satisfied, complain to the Data Protection Board of India.

Your Arc content is already under your control on your phone: you can view, edit, export or delete it in the app. For anything else, write to us. We will reply within 30 days. Usage data and crash reports are not linked to your name or account, so we may be unable to tell which records are yours. Turning the setting off stops new collection, and old records expire as listed in section 7.

## 11. Age

Arc is for people aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly process data of anyone under 18. If you believe a child has sent us data, contact us and we will delete it.

## 12. Health information

Arc's fitness, nutrition, body and calorie numbers are estimates for general wellness. They are not medical advice. See the Terms and Conditions.

## 13. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, Arc will show you the new version and ask for your consent again before the change applies to you. The version number and date are at the top.
